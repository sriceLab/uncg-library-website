Evaluation of the UNCG University Libraries Website

Author: Shemar Rice

Website Evaluation

The UNCG University Libraries website is designed primarily as a starting point for students, faculty, staff, and other library users who need to find information or use library services. From the homepage, the site immediately presents several major areas, including Libraries & Collections, Library Services, Library Spaces, and About the Libraries. The page also includes navigation for Library Home, Library Catalog Search, Chat with a Librarian, Library Research Help, How Do I..., and Library Hours. Based on these components, I predict that the website is intended to support users who may arrive with a specific academic task, such as finding a book or article, accessing a database, locating a course reserve, or getting help with research. It also appears designed to support users who need practical information about the physical library and its services.

One of the strongest components of the website is the catalog search area. The homepage places search options for All, Books, Articles, Videos, and Music directly above a large Search the Catalog field. This organization gives users a clear place to begin when their primary goal is finding library materials. The page also provides an Advanced Search option for users who need more precise results. Below the main search area are direct links to Databases, the Journal A-Z List, Research Guides, and Course Reserves. These links are important because not every research task begins with a general catalog search. For example, a student looking specifically for a scholarly database or a course reading can move directly to the appropriate resource instead of searching through the entire website.

The website appears to facilitate several common tasks. A student can search for books and other materials, locate articles and journals, access databases, find research guides, identify course reserves, communicate with a librarian, and find information about library hours and spaces. The homepage also highlights actions such as borrowing and renewing items and finding librarians by major. These choices suggest that the designers considered both information-seeking tasks and service-related tasks. The site therefore functions as more than an online catalog. It acts as a central gateway to the University's library resources, people, physical spaces, and research assistance.

Overall, the components make the most common tasks relatively easy because important resources are visible without requiring users to understand the entire structure of the library organization. A student who knows that they need a book, article, database, journal, or research guide can identify a relevant option quickly. The prominent search box is especially useful because it allows a user to begin searching without first deciding which department or library service is responsible for the request. However, the large number of choices can also make the homepage feel information-dense. A user who only knows that they "need help with research" may have to decide whether to use Research Guides, Chat with a Librarian, Library Research Help, or another service. In this situation, the website is more effective for users who already understand what they need than for users who are still trying to determine which library resource or service fits their problem.

In conclusion, the UNCG University Libraries website is an effective task-oriented website because it places major research tools and library services within easy reach of its users. Its strongest feature is the way the homepage connects common academic activities to recognizable choices such as catalog searching, databases, journals, research guides, and course reserves. At the same time, the number of navigation choices creates some additional cognitive load for users with less specific goals. If I were evaluating the site for improvement, I would keep the prominent search function while making the distinction between the different types of research help even clearer. The site succeeds because it gives users many paths to useful information, but its next improvement could be helping uncertain users understand which path they should choose.

Primary Tasks Supported by the Website

  • Search for books, articles, videos, and music.
  • Find databases and journals.
  • Use research guides and course reserves.
  • Chat with a librarian or find research assistance.
  • Borrow and renew library materials.
  • Find library hours, spaces, collections, and services.
